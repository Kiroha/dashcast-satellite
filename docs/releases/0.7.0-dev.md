# DashSat 0.7.0-dev — Experimental external app video

Application version code: **8**. DashSat can launch an app on the external Android box,
capture its video with fresh Android consent and send one video track to DashCast over the LAN.
Use **[DashCast 1.9.19-beta](https://github.com/Kiroha/byd-dashcast/releases/tag/v1.9.19-beta)**
for the updated receiver watchdog, performance overlay and cluster lifecycle guard.

Install over DashSat 0.6.0-dev without uninstalling, manually or through **Updates**.
The existing package, independent signing key, pairing, guidance, OTA checks and protocol v1
remain compatible. The universal APK grows to approximately **54 MB** because native WebRTC
is shipped in the companion, retaining arm64-v8a, armeabi-v7a, x86 and x86_64. DashCast does not
ship this native dependency. The exact archive version/SHA-256 is verified before every build,
and publisher/upstream notices are bundled.

## Launch and share an external app

1. Set up while parked. Start the existing DashSat connection.
2. Open **Preview received map** in DashCast, or **Show on cluster** when its projection is ready.
3. In DashSat, open **Video projection · experimental** and choose an app.
4. Tap **Start video sharing** and accept Android's sharing dialog. On Android 14+, choose the
   same app in Android's own chooser; the launch selection cannot verify the actual shared app.
   Earlier Android versions mirror the whole box display. The app opens on the box once capture
   starts; **Open selected app** is also available manually.
5. Stop from the DashSat screen or its ongoing notification while another app is open.

Screen sharing can expose personal content; protected content may be blank. Capture uses one
MediaProjection consent and one VirtualDisplay, resized in place. No invisible second display,
camera/microphone/audio, data channel or remote touch/key injection is implemented.

## Video and guidance stay separate

The existing authenticated, certificate-pinned WSS socket carries signalling; WebRTC DTLS-SRTP
carries video directly over LAN UDP. The native network monitor uses only the authenticated
LAN interface/network handle. No process/default routing change, STUN/TURN, relay, IPv6 or TCP
ICE fallback is enabled. Browser UUID `.local` host candidates are mapped locally to the actual
peer address of that authenticated TLS socket; no DNS lookup is used. A browser port bound to
another car interface may fail and stop video without a mobile-data fallback.

Offer/answer/ICE are bounded and fenced by socket generation and fresh negotiation IDs. Early
video cancellation cannot send a stop for a never-transmitted offer or break guidance. A bounded
video signal quota prevents continuous guidance from starving negotiation while retaining fresh
latest-value guidance and heartbeat deadlines.

Viewer closure, control/LAN loss, capture revoke/pause or persistently hidden content stop capture
and release the encoder, peer, texture helper and virtual display. A new user consent is required
to restart video. No boot capture or unattended restart is promised. The reboot preference still
applies only to guidance. Video does not require notification-listener access.

## Performance goal and remaining acceptance

Start at up to **960×540, 15 fps, 1.5 Mbps**, preserving source aspect ratio. H.264 is preferred
among available hardware codecs; hardware encoder absence causes a controlled unavailable state.
A live static capture refreshes twice per second so the receiver can observe actual decoded frames.
Encoder/stat deadlines stop stalled or unobservable video rather than retaining it indefinitely.

The goal is to reduce sustained car CPU/GPU workload by running the app, capture and encoding
on the box. DashCast must still decode and compose video. Hardware encoding on the box and
codec support in other vehicle apps do not establish WebView hardware decoding or workload savings.
Compare identical idle/local-Maps/external-video scenarios with Overdrive for ten minutes and
thirty sustained minutes, measuring latency, decoder/FPS/drop evidence, CPU/GPU and heat.
**Actual Tbox/vehicle video and CPU/GPU savings remain pending**; local software checks are not
hardware acceptance. See [setup and measurement checklist](../EXTERNAL_VIDEO.md).

The user separately confirmed synchronized official Google Maps guidance on Tbox Ultra 1 /
Android 15 and DiLink 3 / Android 10, without perceptible lag in that test. Stop, reconnect, reboot
and overnight guidance tests remain pending. ABRP guidance remains conditional on usable observations.
