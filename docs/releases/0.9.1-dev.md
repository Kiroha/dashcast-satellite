# DashSat 0.9.1-dev — Video readiness recovery and retained activity

Application versionName: **0.9.1-dev**. Version code: **11**.

An older receiver can deliver an unscoped viewer-unavailable rejection after it has advertised
that a new video viewer is ready. DashSat previously treated that old rejection as a new viewer
closure, leaving an authorized sharing session waiting. Only explicit viewer-ready and viewer-closed
events now change readiness. Existing finite negotiation and frame-progress deadlines remain active;
failed media does not silently wait forever or change guidance freshness.

Use **DashCast 1.9.22-beta** on the vehicle for the corresponding receiver ordering fix and receiver
video history. Existing pairing, certificate pins, settings, generic roundabout guidance and the
development application identity remain unchanged. Install through **Updates** or manually over
0.9.0-dev without uninstalling. No new pairing is required. This is a development-channel APK.

## Checking video on the shared car screen

1. Start the DashSat connection, choose the external application and start video sharing.
2. Grant Android capture consent and leave CarPlay to return to DashCast.
3. Open **Preview received map**. Video starts when that viewer is ready.
4. If no image appears, return to DashSat and inspect **Recent video activity** or choose
   **Copy video diagnostics** before starting another session.

Returning to CarPlay hides the receiver preview, so a live **waiting for DashCast** state there
is expected. The new history preserves what happened while the receiver was visible: capture
creation, hardware encoder setup, transmitted offer, answer application, media connection,
encoded-frame progress or a fixed failure category. Newest events appear first and local session
numbers distinguish authorized sharing attempts. Up to 32 entries and saturating counts are held
in memory only; the video page shows the eight newest entries. ICE contributes to totals only.

Successful control sends remain in the history even if their completion reaches a closed or
replaced capture peer; obsolete callbacks cannot advance or fail the current session. The history
contains no tokens, SDP, routes, notifications, network/device identifiers or raw errors. Neither
a sent offer nor encoded frames confirms a displayed receiver image. Receiver bug reports retain
their own decoded-frame evidence separately.

The same valid Android capture may still wait for or resume a viewer without a second consent or
VirtualDisplay. Stop, revocation, lock, source pause/hiding, control loss and fatal failures still
end sharing and require fresh consent. Video remains one hardware-encoded track over local IPv4
UDP, with no software encoder, audio, remote input, cellular media fallback or new OEM behavior.

## Validation and limits

Independent source checks passed: **201 JVM tests across 32 suites**, debug lint with **zero
errors and warnings**, debug assembly, and guidance/pairing fixture verification. Exact artifact
identity and signing checks are recorded alongside the frozen release assets. Protocol v1 and its
fixtures remain pinned to reviewed receiver commit `784ad22e2ffd40d354b16d0f195cd10794b47454`.
The correction is covered by deterministic ordering, authenticated transport, stale callback,
bounded/privacy history and existing capture lifecycle tests. It fixes a demonstrated code race;
the available vehicle report did not prove that race caused the reported blank video.

Actual Tbox Ultra 1 / Android 15 to DiLink 3 / Android 10 video, installed car WebView compatibility,
hardware decoding, latency and CPU/GPU savings still require vehicle validation. Local checks do
not establish a displayed map or lower vehicle workload. Official Google Maps guidance was
previously confirmed on the real vehicle; video and guidance have independent states.
